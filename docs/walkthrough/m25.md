# M2.5 walkthrough: the compound

What to try, and what to look for, after upgrading the office to M2.5 (the
lair compound, SPEC §9, §12, D21–D23). Allow about 30 minutes. Screenshots
from the #190 gate are in [`docs/screenshots/190/`](../screenshots/190/).

Tick each box as you go; anything that looks wrong is worth an issue, with a
screenshot and what you did.

## 1. Upgrade

1. Take a backup first (see section 10): `cd deploy && docker compose exec backup scripts/backup.sh`.
2. On the server: `scripts/setup.sh --upgrade`. It fast-forwards the clean
   checkout, rebuilds what changed and restarts the office. Robots keep
   running in their runner containers.
3. Open the office.

Look for:

- [ ] The office comes back at `https://<your domain>` and `docker compose ps` shows every service healthy.
- [ ] Your existing floors are now **rooms**, in a row north of the main corridor, each with its repo, desks, robots and seats (migration, #181). A robot that was working before the upgrade is still in the same chair.
- [ ] Nobody lost a login (runners were not touched).

## 2. Genius picker

The first time each person opens the office after the upgrade they pick a genius (#185).

- [ ] The picker opens by itself: six archetypes (scientist, tycoon, general, hacker, diva, mastermind), each with outfit, trim, skin, hair and accessory choices, on a turning preview.
- [ ] It works with the keyboard alone (arrows between choices, Tab between rows, Enter on "Enter the lair").
- [ ] After "Enter the lair" you stand in the lobby as that genius, with your name plate over your head.
- [ ] Settings → "Your genius" changes it later; a colleague's browser shows the change at once.

## 3. Walking the compound

- [ ] **First view**: you start at a room-level framing (about 30 m out, the whole lobby in view), not close behind your shoulder (#190). The wheel zooms in to a close third-person view and out to the whole compound in its mountain.
- [ ] **Turning**: `Z` turns the camera 45° left, `C` 45° right, a right-drag turns freely. `E` never turns the camera; it only interacts (#190; SPEC §9.2 still says Q/E and will be updated).
- [ ] **Walking**: click the floor to walk there (A* through corridors and doors), or use WASD relative to the camera. Your genius faces the way it walks and turns to follow the cursor when standing.
- [ ] **Doors**: room doors slide open as you come near, only for rooms you may enter. A room you may not enter shows a shut door, a plaque with its name and counts (working, waiting, RESTRICTED) and a rock cap; you never see inside.
- [ ] **Plaques**: the brass plaque by each door leans out a little and is readable from the default view (#190).
- [ ] **Quick travel**: `F` lists the rooms you may enter; picking one puts you at its door.
- [ ] **First person**: `V` (or the HUD button) swaps to eye height with mouse look and back, with a short crossfade.
- [ ] **Overview**: zoomed all the way out, the compound sits dug into a mountain with green shoulders, headlands and the sea round it; past the mountain is open sea, not a black void (#190).
- [ ] **Look and feel**: logical zones (lobby, corridors, war room, break room, project rooms), warm tungsten pools against cool rock, plants and clutter at desks: spacious but lived-in.

## 4. Build mode

Owners and admins build, move and delete rooms (#187).

1. Rooms panel → **Add floor…**: name, palette and repo(s), then **Choose a spot…**.
2. The camera zooms out over the compound and a ghost of the room follows the mouse.

- [ ] Size presets S 6×6, M 8×8, L 10×10, or a custom width and depth from 4 to 12 tiles.
- [ ] The ghost is green where the room fits and red where it overlaps or has no route; red shows the server's reason in words and outlines what it hits.
- [ ] The corridor the room would add is previewed. `R` turns the door (Shift+R back), arrows nudge the ghost one tile, `Z`/`C` turn the camera, the wheel zooms.
- [ ] **Build here** (or Enter) starts the build phase: scaffolding, crates, sparks, dust, three henchmen hammering, a countdown plate over the door. Then the scaffolding sinks and the interior rises. The camera goes back to where it was.
- [ ] **Move room…** (in a project room with no running robots) moves it the same way and puts you at the new door. Moving while robots run is refused with a clear message.
- [ ] Deleting a room (Floor settings, from the top bar or the gear in quick travel: archive, then delete) plays a demolition; the room's files go with it.

## 5. Room settings

Room managers change a room's settings (#182, #187).

- [ ] Rooms panel → **Room settings…** opens a docked panel: desk count (4 seats each, up to what the room's size fits) and decor style (ops room, lab, workshop, war room).
- [ ] Every change is previewed live in the scene before Save; Escape or Close drops it.
- [ ] Shrinking below a desk with a seated robot is refused. Seat ids stay stable: adding a desk never moves a robot.

## 6. Blast door, beach and dock

- [ ] In the lobby, walk to the red button right of the blast door and press `E` (or click it). The door unbolts and slides open, the klaxon whoops and the beacons turn, and everyone else within 40 m gets a toast "<name> opened the blast door. It shuts in N s."
- [ ] Walk out onto the beach and along the dock: surf and gulls fade in outside and fade out deep inside.
- [ ] After 60 s (`OFFICE_BLAST_DOOR_SECONDS`) the klaxon warns and the door shuts by itself. The keypad outside opens it again, so nobody is stranded.
- [ ] A colleague in the lobby sees the same door state.

## 7. Henchmen and skins

- [ ] Coding agents are henchmen: yellow jumpsuits with provider-colour trim (Claude orange, Codex teal) and a status light (grey starting, green idle, blue working, orange waiting with a raised hand, red error).
- [ ] They sit still when idle and animate while working; the laptop bubbles show tool calls, edits and tests.
- [ ] Settings → **Henchman skins** (owners and admins): add a rule (for example `provider:codex` → lab coat), see the turntable preview, and watch matching robots change skin in the room at once.

## 8. HUD schemes

- [ ] The HUD is a retro lair control panel (#189): brass frames, rivets, stencil nameplates, lamp-style status chips.
- [ ] It follows your system theme: **night shift** (dark gunmetal) when the system is dark, **day shift** (cream enamel) when it is light. Switch the OS theme and the HUD follows without a reload.
- [ ] HUD panels never cover what you need in the scene at 1920×1080 or 1280×720; with `?stats` the fps panel sits bottom right.

## 9. Graphics quality and performance

Settings → **Graphics quality**: Auto (picked from your GPU), Low, Medium or High (#190).

| Preset | Picked for | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| High | a dedicated graphics card | everything: antialiasing (MSAA), six pooled lamp lights, alarm lights, blob shadows, all decor |
| Medium | integrated graphics (Intel, AMD APUs), or a browser that hides its GPU | no antialiasing, three lamp lights; all decor stays |
| Low | software rendering (no GPU, a VM, CI) | no lamp lights or shadows, no mountain, less decor, flat floors, only rooms near you, 0.75 resolution |

- [ ] The Auto label names the preset it picked, e.g. "Auto (Medium)" on a laptop.
- [ ] Changing the preset applies at once (a change of antialiasing restarts the 3D view for a moment).

**Owner to-do: measure on a real 2020-class laptop iGPU.** The gate machine has no iGPU (see the method below), so the SPEC §11 target is not yet confirmed on real hardware. On a 2020-class laptop (Intel Iris Xe / UHD 620 or AMD Vega 7–8) at 1920×1080:

1. Open `https://<your domain>/office?stats` in Chrome, plugged in, with the browser window maximised.
2. Walk into the busiest room (ideally 20 henchmen working), wait 10 s, then run in the DevTools console:
   `copy(JSON.stringify(window.__regulusPerf.sample(), null, 2))` and paste the result into issue #190 (or a new issue).
3. Repeat zoomed all the way out, and on the beach with the blast door open.
4. Look for: `fps` about 60, `p90` under 20 ms, and the tier the laptop picked (`quality`). If it is not 60, try Low and Medium in Settings and note the difference.

### How the gate measured it (#190)

- **Scene.** A production build of the office harness (`apps/web/dev/robots.html`, built with `apps/web/vite.harness.config.ts`): a 64-tile compound with 12 project rooms, 20 henchmen working in the 12×12 Dev room, 3 nearby rooms with 4 robots each, and 4 humans (you plus 3 walking round). `bun scripts/perf/measure.ts` drives it in Chromium and reads `window.__regulusPerf`.
- **Numbers.** Frame-interval percentiles over 6 s after a 5 s settle; main-thread time per frame (scene update plus render submission); draw calls and triangles of the last frame; texture memory estimated from the textures the visible materials use (RGBA8 plus mip chain); FloorRoom join times from the live office.
- **Profiles.** No iGPU exists on the gate machine (i9-12900K with its iGPU disabled; GTX 1660 SUPER), so the iGPU is approximated, not measured:
  - `gpu`: the GTX 1660 SUPER at 1920×1080, vsync on (what a user sees).
  - `igpu` proxy: 3840×2160 (four times the pixels of 1080p, so per-pixel work weighs as 1080p would on a GPU with a quarter of the throughput, roughly Iris Xe / Vega 8 class) plus Chrome's CPU throttling ×4 on the main thread (via CDP), vsync on. `igpu-uncapped` is the same without vsync, for headroom.
  - `software`: SwiftShader at 1280×800, the CI tier and the floor for any machine.
- **Limits of the proxy.** It cannot reproduce an iGPU's shared memory bandwidth, thermal throttling or driver overheads, and a UHD 620 is weaker than the Iris Xe class it approximates. Hence the owner to-do above.

## 10. Backups

- [ ] `docker compose ps` shows the `backup` service healthy (a backup newer than 26 h).
- [ ] `ls deploy/backups/` lists nightly `office-<UTC time>.db` files; older than 14 days are pruned.
- [ ] Take one now: `cd deploy && docker compose exec backup scripts/backup.sh`.
- [ ] Optional dry run of a restore on a spare machine: `scripts/restore.sh` lists the backups and puts one back (it stops `office` and `backup`, keeps the old database aside, and starts the stack again; robots keep running). Keep `deploy/.env` with the backups: without the same `OFFICE_MASTER_KEY` stored keys cannot be decrypted.
- [ ] Copy `deploy/backups/` and `deploy/.env` off the machine (for example a nightly rsync to a Hetzner Storage Box).
